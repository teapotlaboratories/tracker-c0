Q4/Q5/Q6: modem control FETs

These three N-FETs drive the modem's PWRKEY and RESET (Q4, Q5) and invert its 1.8 V STATUS output (Q6). Q6 has to be fully on with only 1.8 V on its gate, and it sits with 3.3 V on its drain during sleep, so its off-state leakage counts against the one-year battery budget. LCSC had 52 of the DMG1012UW-7 (about 17 boards), Digi-Key had none, and Mouser had no listing.

Diodes' DMN2710UW-7 matches the DMG1012UW-7 on every limit in its datasheet. Its top-view drawing (p.1) has the same pin arrangement, so it drops onto the same SOT-323 land. It also costs a quarter of the LCSC price. Digi-Key is already a supplier in the BOM (for U3).

Copper pulled back from the board edge

The copper-to-edge rule was 0 mm, so DRC never flagged the GND pours that ran right to the outline on all four layers, or the 110 vias whose copper sat 0.10–0.30 mm from the edge. A fab routes the outline through that copper, which exposes the inner planes at the edge, and usually puts the order on hold.

  • Rule set to 0.3 mm and every pour refilled: the GND fills now stop 0.3 mm from the outline on F.Cu, In1, In2 and B.Cu.
  • 104 GND stitching vias moved straight inward by up to 0.22 mm, so their copper is 0.32 mm from the edge. None carries a track.
  • 6 vias removed: five on the right edge between the edge and the J3 shield and J5 mounting pads, and one beside TP29. With the edge rule there is no room for them; the stitching row keeps its 1 mm pitch elsewhere.
  • The LTE antenna feed and the E1 keep-out are unchanged (track geometry identical). The copper diff contains only GND vias and pours.

U1 modem stencil

U1 (SIMCom A7670G, LGA-124) had full paste on every pad. That over-pastes the twenty 2.1 mm ground pads, so the module can float, tilt or bridge. The footprint now carries SIMCom's stencil openings from the A7670G hardware design guide V1.00, Fig. 5 (p.23). Our pad geometry matches SIMCom's Fig. 4 (p.22), so the openings drop straight in.
