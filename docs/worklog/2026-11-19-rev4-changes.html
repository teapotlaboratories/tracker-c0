1

Modem UART shifter shrinks; RESET gets a driver

done

U2 goes from the 4-channel TXB0104 (QFN-14) to the 2-channel TXB0102 (VSSOP-8), carrying only the modem UART. The freed space next to U1 pin 16 holds Q5 with R31/R32: an open-drain RESET driver on GPIO12, copied from the PWRKEY driver.

Why: two of the four channels were unused, and the modem RESET pin was left floating, so a hung modem could only be recovered by cutting its power.

Details: Schematic · Board: U2 and Q5 · Routes

2

Modem STATUS read through an inverter

done

Q6 with R33/R34 turns the modem's 1.8 V STATUS output into a 3.3 V signal on GPIO17, inverted (low = modem on). Both STATUS routes were then tidied to 45° bends.

Why: a 1.8 V high is below what a 3.3 V ESP32 input reliably reads as high.

Details: Board: Q6 · STATUS tidy

3

Modem powered straight from the battery

done

The SY8089 buck (U5, L1, R16, R17, C25) is gone. Q7 (PMV32UP P-FET) switches the battery to the modem, driven by Q8 (2N7002) from CELL_EN. C44/R36 give a ~15 ms soft start (~0.24 A inrush), and R37 drains the modem rail after turn-off.

Why: the buck sat in dropout for most of the battery range, sagged on transmit bursts, and C25 was 47 µF where the datasheet wants 22 pF, so the modem rail took seconds to come up.

Details: Review fixes applied · Fix before ordering

4

GNSS reset pull-up R6 removed

done

R6 (10 k from the always-on 3.3 V to GNSS_RESET) is removed from the schematic and the board (it was first marked not fitted). Firmware drives GPIO1 open-drain, as Quectel's reference does.

Why: with the GNSS rail off in sleep, R6 back-fed the L96 or burned current through the GPIO: about 0.3 mA, ten times everything else asleep.

Details: Fix before ordering · Firmware

5

GNSS backup always on from the battery

done

The L96 backup supply (V_BCKP) is wired straight to the battery: first through JP1 with its pins swapped, then JP1 itself was removed. The backup switch (Q1, Q2, R18, R19, test point TP31) is removed with its two nets; ESP32 GPIO2 is now a no-connect.

Why: the backup was off unless GPIO2 held it on, so every fix was a cold start (~30 s at ~25 mA). Always-on backup costs 7 µA and gives hot starts; it also saves four parts and 7.5 µA of bias.

Details: Should fix · Battery budget

6

Battery connector on top, cable cut-out, logo moved

done

J4 moves from the bottom to the top, so every part is on one side. The bottom-right corner is cut away (rounded corners: 1 mm, and 0.5 mm next to J4) so the cable drops past the board edge; J4 sits 3.3 mm further in. U8, C35, R23 and Q3 moved left, which widens the battery path to the modem to about 3 mm. The top logo, which sat in that corner, moved next to the GPS module, into the space freed by the backup switch and JP1 (upright, and scaled to 62% so it fits the free space, centred between U7 and the TP24 label with 1.15 mm on each side; it uses a new TeapotLogo_Small footprint in the project library). The bottom-silkscreen antenna-option note ("R25+R26 = PCB antenna / R27+R28 = u.FL") is removed, and the CHRG label is now white knockout text like the other labels.

Why: J4 was the only bottom-side part, which meant paying for second-side assembly and a second stencil for one connector.

Part 1

TXB0102, RESET and STATUS

2026-11-17. U2 shrinks from a 4-channel TXB0104 (QFN-14) to a 2-channel TXB0102 (VSSOP-8) that carries only the modem UART. The space it frees next to U1 pin 16 holds a RESET driver, Q5. STATUS reaches GPIO17 through a FET inverter, Q6, instead of feeding 1.8 V into a 3.3 V input.

How the drivers work

Both modem pins are pulled up to VBAT by 50 kΩ inside the module, so the ESP32 must never drive them directly. Q5 copies the existing Q4 PWRKEY circuit: a 1 k series resistor, a 10 k gate pull-down that keeps the FET off while the ESP32 is in reset, and an open drain on the modem pin. Q6 turns the modem's 1.8 V STATUS output into a 3.3 V logic level with the polarity inverted.

Board: U2 and Q5

U2 sits at (128.9, 119.6) mm, rotated so its A pins face the TXD/RXD vias on the left and its B pins face the CELL_TXD/CELL_RXD vias on the right. VCCA and OE are both VDD_1V8, so one straight trace under the body links them. C12 (VCCA) and C13 (VCCB) sit next to their pins.

The DCU pin order puts CELL_TXD and CELL_RXD in opposite order to the incoming traces, so one crossing is unavoidable at any rotation. It is done with layers: the CELL_TXD via moved up its diagonal to (132.7, 118.25), and CELL_RXD passes under it on B.Cu.

Q5 is at (128.85, 116.0), with its drain about 2 mm from U1 pin 16. R31 and R32 are stacked to its left. The reference designators of U2 and the new parts are hidden on the silkscreen, like Q4, R29, R30, C11 and C12 in this area.

U1: why the LABE variant

Neither A7670G variant has GNSS or Bluetooth; the board uses the L96 for GNSS. The difference is the LTE bands. Only the LABE carries B12, B13, B25 and B26, which US networks rely on. In the US the LABE runs on LTE only, because 2G GSM has been switched off there.

Interactive BOM, built from scratch

production/TeapotlabsBWLC1A_InteractiveBOM.html is no longer the iBOM plugin's Rev 3 output. It will not build if the BOM, the CPL and the board disagree on any part, position, side or rotation. The output is a single 1.1 MB file that works offline.

Clicking a line highlights its parts on the board, and clicking a part selects its line. Each part can be ticked off as placed. The bottom view is mirrored, the way you see it when the board is flipped. The board is drawn in KiCad's layer colours, like the images above: front copper red, back copper blue, cream silkscreen on a dark background. It shows every track, via and copper pour, and the silkscreen comes from KiCad's own plotter, so text and dashed lines look exactly as they do in KiCad. Review: fix before ordering

The review as written on 2026-11-18, before any fix. Status tags: fixed on hold open kept, your call. The re-checked tag marks items I confirmed myself on the board and in the datasheet. Everything else is the reviewer's evidence, quoted with its source.

criticalC25 is 47 µF where the buck needs a 22 pF feed-forward capacitorfixedre-checked
What

C25 connects the SY8089 FB node to CELL_BATT, in parallel with R16 (120 k). The datasheet puts Cff = 22 pF there. The part has been there since Rev 3, and both the electrical and PCB reviewers found it independently. It was most likely meant to be 47 pF.

Effect

The modem rail ramps with τ = 120 k × 47 µF ≈ 5.6 s, so it reaches 3.4 V several seconds after CELL_EN instead of in 1 ms. The ESP32 waits awake through every modem wake, and firmware that pulses PWRKEY after 500 ms would hit a half-powered modem. At turn-off, C25 drags FB below its −0.3 V absolute maximum. Loop stability is also likely to suffer (not measured).

Fix

Fit 22 pF C0G for C25. That's a value change only, on the same 0603 footprint. If more output bulk is wanted, add it from CELL_BATT to GND.

SY8089A1AAC.pdf p.1 Fig. 1 and p.7 Table 1 (Cff 22 pF); p.3 FB absolute max. Board: C25 pads on Net-(U5-FB) and CELL_BATT.

highR6 pulls GNSS reset to the always-on 3.3 Vfixedre-checked
What

R6 (10 k) goes from +3V3 to GNSS_RESET (L96 pin 23, ESP32 GPIO1). The L96's own supply (GNSS_3V3) is switched off in sleep, and its regulator actively pulls that rail to ground.

Effect

Whenever GNSS is off, R6 either back-feeds the unpowered L96 through its reset pin or burns 330 µA when the GPIO is held low. The estimate is about 0.3 mA, around ten times everything else in sleep combined. The size of the leak is unmeasured. The pin is also only rated to 3.1 V high.

Fix

Don't fit R6. Drive RESET open-drain from GPIO1, as Quectel's reference does, or move the pull-up to GNSS_3V3. Also keep the L96 UART and EXTINT GPIOs low or high-Z while GNSS is off.

L96-M33.pdf p.15 (RESET VIHmax 3.1 V, active low), p.26 Fig. 8; TPS7A0333DBVR.pdf p.7 (60 Ω active pull-down when disabled).

highCopper runs right to the board edge on all four layerson holdre-checked
What

The copper-to-edge clearance rule is 0 mm, so DRC can never flag this. The GND pours reach the outline, and 128 vias sit 0.1–0.2 mm from the edge.

Effect

The fab will put the order on hold or pull the copper back itself. Inner-plane copper ends up exposed at the routed edge.

Fix

Set the edge clearance to 0.3 mm (0.5 mm if the panel is V-scored), refill, and move the edge via rows about 0.5 mm inward.

bwlc1a.kicad_pro min_copper_edge_clearance: 0.0; Gerber copper extent vs Edge.Cuts (PCB reviewer).

highThe modem paste stencil doesn't follow SIMComon hold
What

The 20 inner 2.1 mm GND pads get full-size paste, and the 80 edge pads get 2.0 × 0.6 mm openings. SIMCom specifies a 2×2 split (about 58 % coverage) on the inner pads and 0.5 × 1.5 mm on the edge pads, with a 0.12–0.15 mm stencil. Several vias also sit within 0.05 mm of U1 pads.

Effect

The modem, the most expensive part on the board, can float, tilt or bridge, which hurts yield.

Fix

Draw custom paste windows on the U1 footprint per SIMCom Fig. 5, and pull the near-pad vias at least 0.15 mm clear.

SIMCom_A7672X_Series_Hardware_Design_V1.04_2023-02_EN.pdf p.25 Fig. 5; F_Paste Gerber apertures (PCB reviewer).

highThe modem supply sags on transmit bursts and strands battery capacityfixed
What

The modem needs 3.4–4.2 V with no more than 300 mV of droop. It draws about 2 A peaks on GSM and 0.5–0.64 A on LTE at full power. The buck is set to 3.87 V, so below a ~3.95 V battery it runs at 100 % duty and simply passes the battery through. The series resistance is about 0.3 Ω (buck FET, L1, FB1, Q3) plus the cell's own. L1 saturates at 1.35–1.5 A.

Effect

GSM bursts work only near full charge. On LTE the modem needs about 3.7 V or more open-circuit, so the bottom of the cell goes unused. A brownout means a reboot and a retry, which costs energy.

Fix

Firmware: lock the modem to LTE only. Hardware, your call:

  • feed modem VBAT from the battery through a low-RDS(on) load switch, which is cheaper and drops less;
  • or use a ≥ 2.5 A buck-boost;
  • or keep the buck and fit an inductor with Isat ≥ 3 A (needs a 4 × 4 mm footprint).
