Are 12 pF load capacitors right for Y1?

No. The 32.768 kHz crystal is cut for a 12.5 pF load. With 12 pF on each side, it sees about 7.5 pF, so the sleep clock runs roughly 74 ppm fast (about 6 seconds a day). Espressif's formula with this crystal's data and this board's stray capacitance gives 22 pF. The formula sets a starting value; Espressif says to confirm it by measuring.

R7 isn't in Espressif's drawing. At 22 Ω against the crystal's 70 kΩ resistance it has no measurable effect, so it can stay.

The formula and the data

Espressif gives the formula on the same page, in the section for the main 40 MHz crystal. It's general crystal-oscillator physics, so it applies to the 32 kHz crystal too: the crystal sees the two capacitors in series, plus the board's stray capacitance. Espressif doesn't give a stray value. It says to adjust after measuring: if the clock runs fast, the load is too small and the capacitors should go up.

The calculation

Stray per side ≈ ESP32 pin 2 pF + pads and ~1.5 mm of trace ≈ 0.4 pF = 2.4 pF
Pin-to-pin stray ≈ 0.3 pF (estimate)
Load seen = (C + 2.4) / 2 + 0.3
For 12.5 pF: C = 2 × (12.5 − 0.3) − 2.4 ≈ 22 pF
Offset ≈ C1/2 × (1/(C0 + load) − 1/(C0 + 12.5)), C1 = 3.4 fF, C0 = 1 pF

Only CL, C1, C0 and the 2 pF pin capacitance come from documents. The 0.4 pF of pads and trace and the 0.3 pF pin-to-pin are my estimates for this layout. The last column shows how much the result moves if the per-side stray is anywhere from 1.5 to 3.5 pF.

The crystal's own tolerance (±20 ppm at 25 °C) adds to every row. 22 pF centres the load. Even with the stray at either end of the range, it stays within ±5 ppm of nominal before that tolerance.

Change made

  • C20 and C22 are now 22 pF: FH 0402CG220J500NT, C0G 0402 ±5 %, LCSC C1555, 980,600 in stock (2026-11-19). Same series and size as the old 12 pF part: only the value and part number changed, nothing on the board moved. ERC, DRC and the production check are unchanged; the BOM changed on this line only.
  • Then measure on board 1, as Espressif asks. Run the RTC from the external crystal, compare it with GNSS time over a day, and trim. If it's still fast, go up a value; if it's slow, go down.
  • Why it matters, and how much: this crystal only keeps time while the ESP32 sleeps. At 12 pF, scheduled wake-ups drift by about 6 seconds a day, until the tracker re-syncs from GNSS or LTE. It doesn't stop the board working, but it's a free fix before the first order.
