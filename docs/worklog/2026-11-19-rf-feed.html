Tracker C0 Rev 4 · LTE antenna feed · U1 pin 60 → E1

Is the antenna feed right?

Mostly yes. The feed is laid out as a coplanar line over ground, and the antenna corner follows TE's layout guide. Solving the real cross-section gives about 45 Ω, not 50 Ω, because the original sizing ignored copper thickness and solder mask. That costs about 0.06 dB, which is negligible. The bigger limit is the board size: the ground plane is shorter than the antenna needs for full low-band efficiency.

TL;DR

  • The trace is built as designed: 0.33 mm wide, 0.21 mm gaps to ground, continuous ground plane underneath, ground vias along both sides.
  • Real impedance ≈ 45 Ω (42–48 Ω depending on the fab's stack-up), not 50 Ω. That costs at most about 0.1 dB, so no layout change. Order with impedance control and PCBWay can trim it to 50 Ω.
  • The antenna corner follows TE's guide. Tune the match (C43/L3) on board 1; TE used 12 nH / 4.7 pF, we fit 10 nH / 3.3 pF.
  • The board is the real limit: about 68 mm of ground plane against TE's suggested 120 mm, so the 700–960 MHz bands will be the weakest. An external antenna on J6 is the way around it.
  • On board 1, use the IPEX bypass (R27/R28 instead of R25/R26, short cable J6 → J7) to compare the PCB trace against a cable.

The feed path

U1 pin 60 → 3.2 mm → R25 (0 Ω) → 7.3 mm → R26 (0 Ω) → 1.9 mm → C43 3.3 pF in series, L3 10 nH to ground → 9.4 mm → E1. About 22 mm of 0.33 mm trace, all on F.Cu. J6 (next to U1) and J7 (next to the matching network) are a bypass for the PCB trace: fit R27 and R28 instead of R25 and R26, and a short IPEX cable from J6 to J7 carries the signal instead of the ~19 mm of trace between them. It still feeds E1 through the same match. In the default build (R25/R26 fitted, R27/R28 not) J6 and J7 are idle. The ESD diode D12 isn't fitted.

Impedance

The design numbers (0.33 mm trace, 0.2 mm gap, 0.1855 mm prepreg of Dk 4.56 to L2) give exactly 50 Ω in the usual thin-copper formula. Those formulas disagree once you add copper thickness, which matters with gaps this narrow. So I solved the actual cross-section with a 2-D field solver, after checking it against two known answers.

Solver check: microstrip of the same width gives 51.5 Ω against 51.4 Ω from the Hammerstad–Jensen formula; thin-copper coplanar gives 49.9 Ω against 50.1 Ω from conformal mapping. Grid 5 µm. The mask thickness and Dk 3.8 are typical values I assumed; PCBWay's actual mask isn't known.

Does 45 Ω matter?

Barely. A 45 Ω section in a 50 Ω path reflects about 6 % of the signal at each end. In the worst case that's 18.7 dB return loss and 0.06 dB of mismatch loss. With the thinnest prepreg in the table (42.7 Ω) it's 0.1 dB. The antenna's own match, about 7–13 dB return loss in TE's data (p.2), dominates by far.

Hitting 50 Ω on the real cross-section takes a narrower trace and a wider gap, for example about 0.26 mm with 0.25 mm gaps (51 Ω in the solver). Ordering with impedance control lets PCBWay do that adjustment with their own stack-up and solver.

What the short ground plane costs

Our ground plane is shorter than TE's smallest curve, so expect the low LTE bands between 698 and 960 MHz to be the weakest, including B12 and B13, which US carriers use for coverage. That comes from the board's 83.75 mm length; no change to the feed can fix it.

The u.FL pair doesn't help here: the IPEX bypass still feeds E1, so it has the same ground plane. If low bands turn out weak on board 1, J6 can also take an external antenna directly (R27 fitted, R25 and R26 removed), which gets around the board-size limit.

What to do

  • Order with impedance control and ask PCBWay for 50 Ω ±10 % coplanar (grounded) on L1, trace 0.33 / gap 0.2 mm referenced to L2, allowing them to adjust the trace width and gap. Without that, expect about 45 Ω, which still works.
  • Tune the match on board 1 with a network analyzer at the U1 side of R25 (C43 and L3, starting from TE's 12 nH / 4.7 pF). This matters more than the line impedance.
  • Compare the PCB trace with the IPEX bypass on board 1: swap R25/R26 for R27/R28, fit a short cable from J6 to J7, and compare signal strength. A small difference confirms the trace (about 45 Ω, under 0.1 dB of mismatch) costs nothing that matters.
  • Check low-band performance, comparing signal strength on a low and a high band. If low bands are weak, an external antenna on J6 is the way around the board-size limit.
  • No layout change is needed before ordering.
